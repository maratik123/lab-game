# Design: World generation rework — hexagonal chunks on a super-lattice

**Issue:** #119
**Date:** 2026-09-14

## Approach

The shipped core generates per coordinate over rhombic chunks. `hexgrid.Dims{Cols, Rows}` maps a cell to its chunk by floor division, and `maze.Generator.Cell(coord)` builds the whole chunk and reads six faces. `Cell` asks a `PrefabClaimer` first, and every border carries one or two portals `[measured a2c3c17:internal/hexgrid/chunk.go:11-45 · Read → Dims{Cols,Rows}, ChunkOf by floorDiv32] [measured a2c3c17:internal/maze/generate.go:21-125 · Read → New(seed, params, claimer); Cell(coord) builds own chunk then reads faces] [measured a2c3c17:internal/maze/portal.go:33-52 · Read → count := boundedDraw(s, 2) + 1]`.

The amended `docs/DESIGN.md` §2.2.2 changes what generation's unit is, not only the chunk's shape. A chunk is generated once and stored with its generation version. Its fabric depends on its type. Its border with an existing neighbour comes from that neighbour's stored map. A per-coordinate `Cell(coord)` that is a function of `(seed, coord)` alone cannot express any of that. So the rework keeps the pure pipeline shared by all generation passes: the key chain, the draw reductions, the spanning-structure algorithms and the cycle pass. It replaces the entry point with a **chunk-level** one that returns a chunk map.

### Key decisions

| # | Decision | Why / rejected alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **The super-lattice geometry.** `hexgrid.Chunk` stays an axial `(Q, R)` pair, now read as a coordinate *on the super-lattice*. Chunk `(i, j)` has its centre at cell `i·(2R+1, −R) + j·(R, R+1)` in axial coordinates. So the super-lattice is itself an axial hex lattice. A chunk's six neighbours use the same six direction deltas as a cell's (`Chunk.Neighbor(d)`), and the existing `ChunkDistance` formula stays correct on it. A chunk holds exactly the cells within hex distance R of its centre, `3R²+3R+1` of them. Each of its six borders is `2R+1` faces, and those faces form one contiguous path from one three-chunk corner to the other. | These are DESIGN §2.2.2's hexagon-of-hexagons tiling written in this package's coordinates. A scratch enumeration confirmed them for small radii before they went into this design. The implementation's own tests establish them `[derived → AC1, AC2, AC5 tests in internal/hexgrid/lattice_test.go]`. Rejected: a new chunk-coordinate type per lattice. `Chunk` already is an axial pair, and a second one would only duplicate `Neighbor` and the distance. |
| D2 | **The mapping is a hand-roll, argued.** The standard library has no hex topology. Of the packages the search found: `github.com/pmcxs/hexgrid` is a 2019 pseudo-version with no hexagon-of-hexagons mapping, no local coordinate and no border enumeration, and its pixel conversions are `float64` `[measured 2026-09-14:https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/pmcxs/hexgrid · WebFetch → no super-hex API, NewFractionalHex(float64), v0.0.0-20190126]`. `github.com/gravitton/hexagon` v1.4.0 likewise has no chunk mapping and no edge enumeration between larger hexagons, and its `FractionalHex` is `float64` `[measured 2026-09-14:https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/gravitton/hexagon · WebFetch → no hierarchical/chunking features; FractionalHex float64]`. Search also returned further `hexgrid` modules described as ports of the Red Blob Games implementation guide. None was inspected, and the guide's own treatment of chunking only links to a notebook by Sander Evers `[measured 2026-09-14:https://www.redblobgames.com/grids/hexagons/ · WebFetch → "Sander Evers has a nice explanation of how to combine small hexagons into a grid of large hexagons"]`. That notebook refused the fetch (see § Open questions). | This is the `AGENTS.md` § Dependency Versions standard for an argued wheel. The mismatch is specific: no package inspected offers the hexagon-of-hexagons mapping or a border's face enumeration, which is the whole requirement. The one primitive such a package could supply is the axial distance, a one-line formula `hexgrid` already carries in `ChunkDistance` `[measured a2c3c17:internal/hexgrid/chunk.go:47-55 · Read → (|dq|+|dr|+|dq+dr|)/2]`. Importing a module for it would add a coordinate type beside `hexgrid.Coord` and a float path the determinism guard would have to police. Escape hatch: `hexgrid.Lattice` is the single seam. An established package that gains an integer super-lattice mapping replaces its method bodies without touching `maze`. |
| D3 | **`Locate` is integer-only and exact.** Solve for the chunk index with the inverse super-basis in `int64`: `i* = ((R+1)q − R·r) / N` and `j* = (R·q + (2R+1)·r) / N`, both floor-divided, with `N = 3R²+3R+1`. Then take whichever of the four chunks `(i*+di, j*+dj)`, `di, dj ∈ {0, 1}`, has its centre within distance R. The local coordinate is the cell minus that centre, computed in `int64`. Its inverse, `At(chunk, local)`, adds in `int64` and converts back, so the round trip is exact over the whole `int32` domain. | `internal/detguard` bans `math` and floats on this path `[measured a2c3c17:internal/detguard/detguard.go · Read → bannedImports includes "math"; bare float32/float64 banned]`. Why four candidates and not a closed form: the candidate set is checkable. In a scratch probe it located every cell exactly once, and a one-candidate mutant of the same probe failed. The tests are what establish it for the code `[derived → AC1 exhaustive and rapid round-trip tests]`. Rejected: rounding in cube coordinates, which needs fractional arithmetic; and searching all chunks near the cell, which does the same job with more candidates. |
| D4 | **The `int32` seam is outside the connectivity contract, and generation stays total there.** `Coord.Neighbor` wraps at the domain's extremes `[measured a2c3c17:internal/hexgrid/coord.go:58-64 · Read → "Arithmetic at the domain's extremes wraps"]`. `2³²` is not a multiple of the super-lattice period, so a face that wraps around the seam joins two chunks that are not neighbours. `Locate` and `At` stay exact everywhere (D3). Only `Center` of a chunk that straddles the seam wraps, and it does so per Go's defined overflow. A `Map` answers faces in its own local frame and never computes an absolute neighbour. What a movement edge does at the seam is that caller's concern. | The seam lies more than 2³¹ cells from the world centre, where gates spiral from (DESIGN §2.2). Rejected: making the seam agree. It would need a modulus the lattice divides, which means changing `Coord`'s width, far outside this issue. |
| D5 | **Border enumeration lives in `hexgrid`, in a chunk's local frame, in path order.** `Lattice.Border(d)` returns the `2R+1` canonical `Face`s between a chunk centred at the origin and its neighbour in direction `d`. They are ordered along the border path, from the three-chunk corner shared with the neighbour in the previous canonical direction to the corner shared with the neighbour in the next one. For `d ∈ {DirE, DirNE, DirNW}` (the directions `FaceOf` treats as canonical) the list is built directly. For the opposite three, it is the opposite direction's list, translated by `Lattice.Offset(d)`, in the same order. So both chunks of a border enumerate identical faces in identical order by construction. | This is topology with no seed, so it belongs to `hexgrid` (KD-38's split). A local frame keeps enumeration clear of the seam (D4). Path order means *consecutive in the list* coincides with *sharing a vertex*. That makes the non-touching rule (D6) a rule over list positions, and the tests check it against an independent vertex predicate, not against the order itself. |
| D6 | **The portal rule.** A border of `L = 2R+1` faces reads two bounds: `lo = ⌈PortalShareLower × L⌉` and `hi = ⌈PortalShareUpper × L⌉`, using `decimal.Ceil` `[measured a2c3c17:go doc github.com/shopspring/decimal Decimal.Ceil → "Ceil returns the nearest integer value greater than or equal to d"]`. From the border's own stream (`borderKey`, unchanged `[measured a2c3c17:internal/maze/seed.go:112-119 · Read → canonical-ordered chunk pair]`), the rule draws `count = lo + boundedDraw(s, hi−lo+1)`. It then places `count` pairwise non-consecutive positions using the standard bijection: shuffle positions `0..L−count` from the same stream, take the first `count`, sort them ascending (`sort.Ints`), and add each one's rank. `New` refuses: either share outside `[0, 1]`; lower above upper; `lo < 1`, because a border with no portal breaks world connectivity; and `hi > R+1`, because more non-touching positions do not exist on a path of `L` faces. Each rejection names the input it refuses. | `docs/DESIGN.md` §2.2.2 fixes the count range, the rounding up and non-touching within one border. The bijection is exact and uniform over non-touching placements with no rejection loop, and it is integer-only. Rejected: rejection sampling of placements, which has an unbounded draw count. Also rejected: a greedy skip-next-face walk, which biases toward the start of the path. The border's inputs are seed, generation inputs and the two chunks only: no chunk type, no third chunk `[derived → AC8 test]`. |
| D7 | **Island candidates for the hexagon.** A candidate is a local cell at distance ≤ R−1 from the centre, since a border cell has at least one face leaving the chunk. In a gate chunk the centre is also excluded. The target keeps KD-40's denominator: `round(IslandShare × 3R²+3R+1)` using the existing `floor(x+½)` rounding. The connectivity guard stays unchanged, flooding over the chunk-induced subgraph from a border cell, which is never an island `[measured a2c3c17:internal/maze/island.go:22-82 · Read → candidates non-border, shuffled, guard flood from firstBorder]`. `New` checks capacity against the *gate* chunk's smaller count, `3R²−3R`, so one valid input is valid for every chunk type. The degenerate-shape rejection goes away: at R ≥ 6 a chunk always has non-border cells `[measured a2c3c17:internal/maze/params.go:55-58 · Read → capacity == 0 && IslandShare.IsPositive() rejection]`. | Implements §2.2.2 as amended (islands never touch a border; the gate cell is never an island; D29, D30). The gate centre stays reachable because the spanning structure spans every non-island cell `[derived → AC10 test]`. Rejected: checking capacity per chunk type at generation time. It would turn a generation-input error into a per-call error that `New` could have refused once. |
| D8 | **`maze.ChunkType` is a closed Go enum over an open persisted set.** `ChunkTypeFabric` and `ChunkTypeGate`, with a **zero value that is not a type**. `Generate` refuses the zero value and any value it does not know, naming it. `String()` renders `fabric` / `gate`. | A zero-valued `ChunkType` field forgotten by a caller would otherwise silently generate fabric, and the gate's centre could become an island. Post-MVP types (boss area, ruins; DESIGN §2.2.4) add members. `exhaustive` runs with `default-signifies-exhaustive` `[measured a2c3c17:.golangci.yml:44-45 · Read → default-signifies-exhaustive: true]`, so a switch over `ChunkType` must be total or carry a `default`. |
| D9 | **The chunk-level entry point.** `New(seed int64, params Params) (*Generator, error)` validates once. `(*Generator).Generate(ch hexgrid.Chunk, typ ChunkType, neighbors ...Map) (Map, error)` builds one chunk in a pinned pass order: islands (island stream) → algorithm draw → spanning structure → extra passages → the six borders in canonical direction order. A border comes from the matching neighbour's map when one is supplied, otherwise from the portal rule. The `Map` value exposes `Version() int32`, `Chunk() hexgrid.Chunk`, `Type() ChunkType` and `Faces(local hexgrid.Coord) ([6]FaceState, bool)`. `ok` is false outside distance R. A border face reads the border's state. `Generate` refuses a supplied map that is not adjacent to `ch`, a map generated at a different radius, and two maps for the same neighbour, naming each. Its result does not depend on the order of `neighbors`. `(*Generator).CellSeed(c hexgrid.Coord) uint64` keeps the per-cell content seed that `Cell.Seed` carried `[measured a2c3c17:internal/maze/generate.go:32-39 · Read → Cell{Prefab, Faces, Seed}]`. `Cell` and `chunksConsulted` are deleted. | DESIGN §2.2.2 makes a chunk's map the stored unit and its neighbours' stored maps a generation input. Taking neighbour `Map`s rather than raw border slices lets the generator check adjacency and radius, so a border cannot land on the wrong side. It also leaves the stored encoding entirely to #29, as the spec's second key decision requires. Rejected: an exported `Border` value with a public constructor. It would pin a border's in-memory shape as the storage contract this task does not own. Rejected: keeping `Cell(coord)` beside `Generate`. It cannot be a function of `(seed, coord)` once type and stored borders are inputs (clean break, `AGENTS.md` § API Stability). |

Rejected: tying the tag to `Version` by formatting, for the same reason. |
| D11 | **The prefab hook is removed.** `PrefabClaimer`, `New`'s claimer argument, `Cell.Prefab` and `FaceDeferred` go `[measured a2c3c17:internal/maze/prefab.go:5-19 · Read → type PrefabClaimer interface] [measured a2c3c17:internal/maze/algorithm.go:71-82 · Read → FaceDeferred FaceState = iota]`. `FaceState` becomes `FaceWall` (zero) and `FacePassage`. ## Risks

- **Intermediate commits must stay green while the core is swapped in place.** Each of subtasks 2–5 changes a shape that every `maze` test builds on. Mitigation: the order removes the hook first (2), so the lattice swap (3) has nothing prefab to adapt. Subtasks 3–5 re-mint `cells.golden` as an interim artefact, and only subtask 7 fixes its final shape. A subtask that cannot leave `go test ./internal/maze/ ./internal/hexgrid/` green stops and reports instead of carrying a red tree into the next one `[derived → per-subtask gate in the Group A spawn contract]`.
- **A golden re-minted without reading it passes forever.** Mitigation: the island cross-check test (every golden island cell walled on all six sides) survives into the final golden. So does a new cross-check: every portal position line lies in `[lo, hi]` and holds no consecutive pair. Both re-derive their facts live from the generator, not from the file `[derived → TestChunksGolden_* cross-checks]`.
- **`derive.golden` should not move, and a move is a finding, not a re-mint.** The key derivation takes chunk coordinates as plain `int32`s and reads no chunk shape `[measured a2c3c17:internal/maze/seed_test.go:21-70 · Read → deriveGoldenLines over worldKey, cellKey, cellSeed, chunkKey, borderKey only]`. Mitigation: subtask 7 re-runs it and stops if it differs.
- **The non-touching rule verified by the property that built it.** Path order (D5) makes "consecutive" and "shares a vertex" coincide. A test that checks consecutiveness only proves the enumeration agrees with itself. Mitigation: AC7's test decides vertex sharing independently. Two distinct faces `{x,y}` and `{u,v}` share a vertex exactly when `{x,y,u,v}` lies inside one triangle of three mutually adjacent cells. The test is seen RED against a placement mutant that allows adjacent positions `[derived → TestPortals_NoTwoPortalsOfABorderShareAVertex]`.
- **Overflow and division panics in the lattice arithmetic.** `N = 3R²+3R+1` overflows `int64` near the top of `int32` radii, and a wrapped divisor can be zero. Mitigation: D12 bounds `maze` inputs, and D13 makes every `Lattice` method total outside them. A totality test calls every method at `math.MinInt32`, `−1`, `0`, `MaxRadius+1` and `math.MaxInt32` radii with extreme coordinates `[derived → TestLattice_TotalAtExtremeRadii]`. The panic index stays empty `[measured a2c3c17:ai-docs/panic-index.md · Read → table row "— — —"]`.
- **The determinism guard's map-range ban.** Chunk graphs and edge sets use maps today. ## Test Design

Every claim in this section is about a test this task writes: `[derived → the test named]`.

### `internal/hexgrid` (subtask 1)

- **`TestLattice_LocateRoundTripsAndPartitions` (AC1)** — exhaustive over a region of several chunk diameters around the origin at R ∈ {6, 7, 9}. `Locate` returns a local coordinate at distance ≤ R. `At` returns the original cell. A brute-force scan over a wide chunk window finds exactly one chunk whose centre lies within R, and it equals `Locate`'s. Every cell `At(ch, l)` for `l` in `LocalCells()` locates back to `(ch, l)`, and `LocalCells()` has no duplicate and contains exactly the offsets within R.
- **`TestLattice_LocateRoundTripsFarFromOrigin` (AC1)** — `rapid` over the whole `int32` coordinate domain, the seam extremes included: `At(Locate(c)) == c`.
- **`TestChunk_SixSymmetricNeighboursJoinedByAFace` (AC2)** — over a window of chunks: six distinct neighbours; `n.Neighbor(d.Opposite()) == ch`. Brute force over cells: two chunks are neighbours exactly when some cell of one has a face-neighbour in the other.
- **`TestDistance_EqualsLatticeStepCount` (AC3)** — breadth-first step counts on the open lattice from several sources over a small region equal `Distance`; a hand-computed table; symmetry; no overflow at the extremes. Pairs straddling chunk borders at radii 6 and 9 give the same values as pairs within a chunk at equal step count.
- **`TestLattice_BorderHasTwoRPlusOneFacesInOneOrderFromEitherSide` (AC5)** — at R ∈ {6, 7, 9}, for every direction: the list has `2R+1` faces; its set equals a brute-force enumeration of faces joining the chunk to that neighbour; translating the neighbour's `Border(d.Opposite())` by `Offset(d)` gives the identical list, order included; consecutive entries share a vertex under the independent predicate (Risks). The six borders together are every face leaving the chunk, each once.
- **`TestLattice_TotalAtExtremeRadii`** — every method at `math.MinInt32`, `−1`, `0`, `math.MaxInt32` and past `MaxRadius` returns without panicking (Risks).
- `ChunkDistance`'s existing tests stay; the `Dims` tests are deleted in subtask 3.
